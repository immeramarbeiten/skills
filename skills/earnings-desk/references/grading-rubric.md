# Professor's report card — 0 to 5 stars, half-star increments

The two headline grades are *operating/income-statement quality* and *balance-sheet resilience*. They grade **this quarter in the context of the company's stage, history, peers and forward trajectory** — not stock cheapness, short-term price reaction or an automatic buy rating. Weights below are *decision aides*, not a mechanically averaged model. If the business is a bank or insurer, use the sector playbook before applying ratios.

## Grade meanings

| Stars | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 5.0 | Exceptional performance or resilience, high confidence in durability, demonstrably strong vs peers/stage |
| 4.0–4.5 | Strong, with limited material concerns |
| 3.0–3.5 | Mixed/adequate relative to stage and peers; meaningful trade-offs |
| 2.0–2.5 | Weak: deterioration or substantially disappointing fundamentals |
| 1.0–1.5 | Serious fragility or significant underperformance |
| 0.0–0.5 | Acute distress / viability concern, with verified evidence |
| N/R | Insufficient financial evidence to grade credibly |

## A. Income statement and operating performance

Rough diagnostic weights: **30% demand/revenue quality** (organic/volume, cohorts, mix), **30% profitability and incremental margins** (gross/operating, leverage, one-offs), **20% growth sustainability** (retention, backlog, bookings, RPO, orders, guide), **20% economic quality of earnings** (GAAP vs adjusted, SBC, tax, share count, extraordinary gains). Include multiple quarters and at least 2 relevant peers if available. Do not make an unsupported exact claim that a given peer is the only correct comparator.

Questions: Did growth accelerate? At what margin cost? Was the beat from a sustainable driver? How does the margin trajectory compare with the competitive set? Is revenue concentration, pricing or customer mix eroding?

A nonprofitable growth company may earn 4+ if unit economics, revenue durability and operating leverage improve convincingly, with a plausible runway. A profitable incumbent may earn 2.5 if organic business is eroding despite headline earnings. A net-loss company's profits should never be described as positive just because the trend improved.

## B. Balance sheet and resilience

Rough diagnostic weights: **30% liquidity + runway**, **25% debt/lease/commitment coverage**, **20% cash conversion + working capital** (cash flow diagnostic, *not balance-sheet line item*), **15% capital commitments/off-balance-sheet exposure**, **10% capital allocation + dilution**. Cash, restricted cash, marketable securities, net cash/debt, interest coverage, debt maturities, pension, leases, deferred revenue, and the suitability of capital returns matter.

For banks: ordinary debt is funding, so use CET1, liquidity coverage, funding costs, NPL, provisioning and regulatory capital. For insurers: solvency/regulatory capital, reserve development, combined ratio and investment risk. For capital-intensive industrials: backlog, asset quality, covenant/capex coverage and cyclicality.

## Report card presentation

`Income statement: ★★★★☆ 4.0/5 — above-peer growth and expanding incremental margin; weaker GAAP earnings quality due to [source-backed item].`

`Balance sheet: ★★★½☆ 3.5/5 — substantial liquidity; deduct for looming maturities and elevated capex commitments.`

Show: `Strongest evidence | Main deduction | Peer/stage benchmark | What would lift score next quarter | Confidence`. Define peers explicitly and note comparability limitations. Numbers are never *evidence* without a cited source.

## Avoid grading traps

- **Beat ≠ strong quarter:** a stock may beat depressed consensus as the business slows.
- **Strong quarter ≠ good stock:** valuation and expectations still matter.
- **Positive FCF ≠ healthy balance sheet:** compare debt maturity, cash restrictions and working-capital effects.
- **Lower capex ≠ positive:** underinvestment can be bad; higher capex can be accretive but raises funding risk.
- **One-off EPS gains/losses ≠ operating change:** separate adjustments and cash implications.
- **Peer group ≠ one metric:** growth alone never defines software quality, and margin alone never defines retail quality.
